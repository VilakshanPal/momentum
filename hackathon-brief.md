# Brief: "No Hands: The Wispr Challenge" hackathon

## How to use this brief

This is a complete summary of research done on the hackathon page, its ideas page and its submission form, plus the conclusions reached so far. Read it fully, then help me ideate on what to build. One important correction: the previous assistant guessed my role and daily work from tooling it saw in my workspace, and that guess was wrong. Do not assume anything about my job. Ask me what I do and what slows me down in my work before suggesting ideas.

## The core premise

Build a tool that fixes a real, recurring problem in your own work. The twist is that the building process must be done by voice. Wispr Flow is a hold-a-key-and-talk dictation app that turns speech into polished text wherever your cursor is, in any app. You use it to speak your prompts, specs, docs, messages and commit notes into whatever AI tools or builders you use. The keyboard is allowed for shortcuts and small fixes. Judges look at how much of your writing went through Flow.

The page frames the problem with three examples:
- The report you rebuild from the same spreadsheets every Monday.
- The WhatsApp questions you answer with the same three replies.
- The CRM nobody updates because the write-up takes longer than the call.

## The two tracks

You pick exactly one. The submission form has a plain radio button with two options and nothing verifies your role. The rule on the page is: Builders if you write code, No-Code if you don't.

### Track 1: Builders (for developers)
A working software product that solves a problem in your own work. You dictate requirements and follow-ups into coding tools (Cursor, Claude, etc.) and let them write the code. Listed audience: full-stack developers, AI engineers, mobile developers, DevOps engineers.

### Track 2: No-Code (for every other role)
A working tool for your own job made with an AI app builder or an AI assistant, no coding needed. Listed audience: marketers, product managers, sales, founders, designers, HR.

What counts as a No-Code entry, per the FAQ:
- An app made with an AI app builder like Lovable.
- A reusable assistant, such as a custom GPT or a Gemini Gem shared by link.
- A workflow in Zapier, Make or Notion that runs when a judge triggers it, for example from a form.
- A chat thread or a one-off prompt does NOT count.
- Judges must be able to open and use it without paying or asking for access. Share a public link. If it needs a sign-in, include a test login and sample input in the description.

## Bonus categories

- Most Wispr usage: upload your Wispr usage card each week. Ranked by total words dictated during the challenge, in any app. Measured from the desktop app under Insights, then Your usage. Winners' numbers are confirmed with Wispr.
- Social, build in public: post progress with #NoHandsChallenge, tag WeMakeDevs and Wispr Flow, and add the post link to your weekly check-in.

## The four-step process the page describes

1. Register. Set up Wispr Flow first, and make your very first dictation the start of your project: say out loud the problem in your work you want to fix and what the tool should do.
2. Build with Flow. Pick a track and talk your way through it: the spec to your AI tools, the docs, the messages.
3. Share every week. Upload your Wispr usage card and post your progress. Weekly winners every Monday.
4. Submit on the website by 30 October, 11:59 pm IST.

## What the submission form asks for (7 required fields)

1. Project name.
2. Track (Builders or No-Code).
3. Link to the working project. Must be live and openable without signing up for anything. This is what the project gets listed and opened by.
4. Demo video, up to two minutes. Unlisted YouTube or Loom is fine.
5. About the project: a few lines on the problem, who it is for, and how you used Wispr Flow.
6. Wispr usage card: a screenshot from the desktop app (Insights, Your usage, Share). One image, JPG/PNG/WebP/GIF/AVIF, up to 5 MB.
7. Feedback on Wispr Flow: what worked, what got in the way, what you want Flow to do next. Goes to the Wispr team.

Submissions can be edited until the deadline, so track choice and links are not locked in early.

## The organisers' sample submission (avoid copying this)

Project: "Call to Follow-up", Track 2 No-Code, Sales.
- Link: a Lovable app that opens without sign-in, with sample call notes on the first screen so a judge can try it in a minute.
- Video: 1:52, unlisted on YouTube. A real call ends, the person holds the Wispr key and says what happened, and the follow-up email plus CRM note appear. They dictate two fixes and send.
- Usage card: 21,480 words dictated between 16 and 30 October.
- About: "I sell to clinics, and after every call I lose half an hour writing the follow-up and updating the CRM. This is for reps on back-to-back calls. I built it in Lovable by talking through each screen with Wispr Flow, then tested it on my notes from 14 real calls. It now drafts both in under a minute."

This sample is a very good template for what a winning submission looks like: a narrow, personal pain; a tool that opens instantly with sample data preloaded; a demo that shows voice in the loop; a concrete real result (tested on 14 real calls, drafts in under a minute).

## The organisers' example ideas per role (from the ideas page)

These are explicitly "ideas to get you started, not a list to pick from". Entering one of these verbatim means competing against many near-identical entries.

Builders track:
- Full-stack developers: a billing app for a kirana shop that takes the day's orders in the language the shop speaks. Show the working app and the bills it made for a real day of sales.
- AI engineers: an inbox sorting agent that reads your mail and drafts the replies worth sending. Show the agent and a morning of mail it sorted.

No-Code track:
- Marketers: a reusable campaign brief generator. Show a brief made with it.
- Product managers: a PRD writer that starts from call notes. Show a PRD from a real call.
- Sales: a follow-up writer that turns call notes into the email and the CRM update (same as the sample submission).
- Founders: an investor update writer that works from what you say happened this month.
- Designers: a design feedback collector that turns scattered comments into one list of changes.
- HR: an onboarding planner that builds a new hire's first two weeks from the role.

The common pattern in every example: dictate the context, let the tool produce a draft, run it on real inputs from your work, then dictate corrections until it is right. Every one ends with "Show: the working tool, and one real result it produced."

## What was concluded so far in the earlier conversation

1. The track is self-declared. Nothing on the site checks your job. The only guidance is "Builders if you write code, No-Code if you don't."
2. The earlier assistant recommended No-Code on the assumption that I am a founder/ops person who builds with AI assistants rather than writing code by hand. That assumption about my role was wrong and should be re-examined with me directly. The reasoning that still holds regardless of role: Builders will be crowded with professional engineers, No-Code is judged against non-technical professionals, and both tracks can win the overall Top 3.
3. One caveat raised: if the deliverable looks like a code repository, a judge may feel it belongs in Builders even if entered as No-Code. Whatever track is chosen, the thing a judge opens should look and behave like a tool with sample input preloaded, not a repo or a chat thread.
4. The "solo" rule means colleagues cannot join on one entry. Each person registers separately with their own problem.
5. Ideas floated earlier (based on the wrong role assumption, so treat as examples of the shape, not as the shortlist): a Monday report builder that turns a spreadsheet plus a dictated request into a weekly report; a proposal/scope-of-work email writer from discovery call notes; a hiring screener that scores pasted candidate profiles against a rubric for a role actually being hired.
6. Two things to do immediately regardless of the idea: install Wispr Flow via the promo link before registering, since the registration itself is meant to be the first dictation; and start posting with #NoHandsChallenge from week one, because weekly prizes reward building in public.

## What makes an entry score well (synthesised from everything above)

- A narrow, genuinely personal recurring pain, stated in one sentence.
- A tool a judge can open from a public link and try in under a minute, with sample data already on the first screen.
- One real result: it was run on real inputs from your actual work and the output is shown.
- A demo video under two minutes that shows voice driving the build or the use.
- High Wispr word count, which comes naturally from dictating specs, docs, messages and corrections for two weeks.
- Weekly check-ins with the usage card and a public post, to be in the running for Monday prizes.
- Not a copy of the organisers' examples or sample submission.

## Questions to ask me before ideating

- What is my actual role and what does a normal week of my work look like?
- Which repetitive task costs me the most time or annoys me most?
- Do I write code by hand, or do I build things by instructing AI tools?
- Which AI tools and builders do I already have accounts on?
- Who else would use the tool, and what real input data do I have to test it on?
- Which track feels right to me after answering the above?
